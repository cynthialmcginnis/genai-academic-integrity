The tension

Threat or tool? Both. Your use decides.

Bypasses learning

Unauthorised content generation. The tool produces the thing you were supposed to learn how to produce.

Enhances learning

The tool helps you think, practise and check. The learning stays in your head.

Quick count. Have you used GenAI for coursework this term?

What the field recommends

Teach GenAI and integrity together.

The European Network for Academic Integrity says staff and students need guidance on what these tools do well and where they fail. Students also need real practice using them ethically.

Meet GenAI inside a lesson on integrity, and you link the tool with good practice. Meet it at 2 a.m. before a deadline, and you link it with shortcuts.

The framework

Six values hold it up.

The International Center for Academic Integrity names six fundamental values. Each one maps straight onto how you use GenAI. Pick one.

Value 1 of 6

Honesty

Say what is yours and what is not.

Any use of GenAI gets acknowledged and made transparent. Small help still counts as help.

Example

GenAI drafted your outline. You wrote every paragraph. Your submission says both things.

Ask the room

Where is the line between getting help and giving away authorship?

Value 2 of 6

Trust

GenAI is not a source. It hallucinates.

It produces accurate, useful output much of the time. You still cannot assume any given answer is reliable. Analyse it before you use it.

Example

Ask a chatbot for five references on your topic. Some will look perfect and not exist. Check each one in the library database.

Ask the room

How would you verify a chatbot's claim in under two minutes?

Value 3 of 6

Fairness

Clear rules, applied the same way to everyone.

Guidelines on when GenAI can and cannot be used must be available and applied consistently. That holds for students and staff, in every class, programme and discipline.

Example

Course A bans GenAI on exams. Course B requires it in a lab. Both are fair, as long as each states the rule up front.

Ask the room

What happens to fairness when a policy is vague?

Value 4 of 6

Respect

Use GenAI to strengthen learning. Never to skip it.

Respect the learning process, your own potential, and the people around you. Take pride in your own contribution, and be open about the tool's.

Example

Asking GenAI to quiz you on regression builds the skill. Asking it to do the regression homework skips the skill.

Ask the room

Which of your assignments would lose its point if a chatbot did it?

Value 5 of 6

Responsibility

You own the output. GenAI cannot.

This is a real difference between GenAI and human work. A model cannot answer for its errors or bias. You check it for accuracy and bias because your name carries it.

Example

Your report cites a statistic GenAI invented. The error is yours, the same way a signed report is yours no matter who drafted it.

Ask the room

Who is accountable when a GenAI-written answer is wrong?

Value 6 of 6

Courage

The shortcut is easy. Learning takes nerve.

It takes courage to learn new tools. It takes more to keep using them ethically when a shortcut would be faster.

Example

An hour stuck on a proof builds something a pasted answer never will. The struggle is where the skill forms.

Ask the room

When did struggling with a problem teach you more than an answer would have?

One standard for staff and students

Instructors show their work too.

When staff acknowledge their own GenAI use and explain why, students see what acceptable use looks like. The two processes mirror each other.

Instructor building an assignment

  1. Ask GenAI for possible essay questions
  2. Review the list
  3. Select the strongest
  4. Revise them into the final assignment
  5. Tell the class how GenAI helped

Student writing the essay

  1. Brainstorm key points with GenAI
  2. Research each point yourself
  3. Write your own argument
  4. Verify every claim
  5. Acknowledge the GenAI use

What the framework asks of you

Three habits make it work.

Transparency

Say when and how you used GenAI. Staff model it first.

Looks like: an acknowledgment statement on every assignment that used it.

Clear guidelines

Rules may differ from course to course. Each course states its own.

Looks like: reading the syllabus policy before you open a chatbot.

Critical thinking

Pair every use with analysis. GenAI spreads misinformation and bias when nobody checks.

Looks like: treating output as a draft to interrogate, never a verdict.
